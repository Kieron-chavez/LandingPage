The problem

Your expansion process is running on vibes — and the stakes are catastrophic

Most gym owners expanding to a second or third location do it the same way: drive around a neighborhood, check Google Maps for nearby gyms, and trust their instincts. Maybe they ask a commercial broker who gets paid on commission. Maybe they pull a demographic PDF from a website built in 2014.

That worked fine when margins were comfortable. But you're signing a 5–10 year lease. You're committing hundreds of thousands in build-out costs. You're betting your existing locations on a hunch about traffic patterns and income levels in a zip code you visited twice.

"I thought I was opening in an underserved area. Turns out I just couldn't see the competitors from the highway."

One location that underperforms doesn't just bleed cash — it pulls attention, staff, and capital away from the gyms that are actually working. For a 2–5 location operator, that's an existential threat.
